# YC X Launch Desk: core knowledge

## Why a launch on X works or does not
A new post is first shown to a small test audience. What those people do (stop, watch, comment, share, leave) decides whether it is shown to more. Build everything for the first small crowd, not the eventual large one. This is a working model, not a published rule. Treat it as a hypothesis.

## What ranking seems to reward (check current public documentation before relying on any of it)
- Early engagement from people who fit the topic.
- Replies and conversations. The author replying counts as part of the conversation.
- Watch time on native video, and quotes with an opinion attached.
- Links in the post body may reduce reach on some surfaces. Decide link placement on purpose: in the post, in a reply, or in the profile.
- Negative signals (mutes, blocks, reports, "not interested") hurt. Never provoke them for engagement.
Label every ranking claim as a hypothesis. Never present an outside view or conversion figure as the owner's.

## Principles
1. **One KPI you can count the next morning** (sign-ups, orders, qualified replies). Views are a diagnostic, not a goal.
2. **Check the destination before blaming the creative.** A broken page, offer or tracker sinks the best post.
3. **Small and fresh beats big and tired.** A fitting account with a rested audience beats a huge account that posts sponsored content daily.
4. **Audience overlap beats follower count.** Score fit before reach.
5. **First line and first five seconds decide most of it.** Most viewers never reach the middle.
6. **A quotable line travels.** One sentence built to be reposted with an opinion.
7. **Quiet window.** Keep the owner's other posting quiet around the slot so the launch is the only story.
8. **Honesty.** True numbers only. Every claim checkable in the replies. No manufactured urgency.
9. **Quotes are natural, not scripted.** In each person's own voice, never identical. People decide freely. No payments without the owner's written approval.
10. **No network is not no launch.** Replies, a few real notes, and a post built for strangers can carry a first launch.

## The claim (from the market, not the product)
Start with the market and forget the product for a moment. Write a market card: what the buyer loves, hates and wishes existed; one strangely specific grievance that is named, dated and checkable; the enemy (the old way, a habit, a broken norm); the highest-status outcome; the buyer's exact phrases; the argument already running in the market; a format skeleton that already worked elsewhere and the small part you will change. Then write a claim bigger than the product that rides inside that argument. A claim that makes the owner slightly nervous, but is true, is often the right one.

## The launch spine
Introduction (the bold claim), problem (plain words, no jumps), agitate (cost, embarrassment, status, and a door left open for disagreement), solution (the mechanism shown, not described, with the strongest outcome). This order holds for the post and the video.

## Launch post anatomy
Line one: the claim or the strangest true moment. Line two: what it is, for whom, in plain words. Then proof (a number or named fact with a source). The link above "Show more" or in the planned reply. Native video that opens on the proof. Nothing about "the journey".

## Video basics
Hook in the first five to seven seconds; plan for the first two. Put the most impressive moment first. Captions on. Show the product doing the thing. Something changes on screen every couple of seconds in the open. Talking head alone in only a small share of shots. One idea per scene. One quotable line. End on the strongest sentence, with the product inside the close, not a dead logo card. Every beat keeps answering three viewer doubts: is this what I clicked; is this for me; is it worth staying.

## Follower scoring (for notes the founder sends by hand)
Fit 0 to 3 (cares about the topic), reach 0 to 3, relationship (cold, seen, replied, friend). Shortlist up to 200. Each note is written by hand and sent by the owner only. No bulk DMs, ever.

## Creator two-minute check
Open the account's last ten posts (leave out the pinned one). Note the date span, how many are on topic, and whether the audience engages with posts like this. Do they post sponsored or product content daily? Any recent controversy? Is it a fit for the claim? Stale accounts (newest post older than about 30 days) go to watch. Log one line. Skip on any failed check.

## Wave pacing
Run sheet at T0 plus five, fifteen and sixty minutes: what to look at, what to reply to, what to resist. A second beat (a clip, an honest update, a demo reply) is drafted in advance and used only if something true and new exists.

## Retro
One page at three days: KPI against the written target, what the first viewers did, which lines were quoted, who moved it, what broke, three keeps, three cuts, three tries, next angles. Read the diagnosis in order: destination and tracking, then the first line and first five seconds, then the claim, then the people plan, then timing.

## The byte-locked evaluator
yc-x-launch-desk-test-of-300 is the owner's own prompt, kept byte for byte. Never edit it. The checks are never applied to it (the scripts skip it automatically).

## Never
Never post, quote, reply, like, repost, schedule or DM for the owner. Never automate sends. Never pay anyone without the owner's written approval. Never invent stats, caps or deadlines. Never judge by views alone.

Revision cap: two revision rounds per piece of work. After round 2, stop, show the best version with its open flags, and ask the owner how to go on.
