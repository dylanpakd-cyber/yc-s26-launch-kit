# SKILL: Test of 300 launch evaluator

Use when: the owner has a launch draft (claim, script or video concept) and wants it judged before filming or posting. Run it at the harsh-read gate (T-24h), and again on any rewrite. Ask the owner to fill every bracketed slot. Any slot left empty is scored "insufficient signal", not guessed.

## Prompt (use verbatim)

<context>
I am about to launch a product on X and I want it evaluated before I commit to filming or posting.

On X, the algorithm first shows a new post to roughly 300 people and evaluates everything they do: do they stop scrolling, watch to the end, comment, or leave? If it passes, it unlocks a wider audience. If it fails, the launch dies at 300 impressions, no matter how much was spent.

I want my launch judged against this reality and the principles below, without sugarcoating.

My launch:

- Product, in one sentence: [WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT DOES, IN PLAIN LANGUAGE]
- The buyer (ICP): [THE EXACT PERSON I AM SELLING TO — role, company stage, what they own]
- The opening line / bold claim: [THE FIRST THING SAID IN THE LAUNCH — the line that must stop the scroll]
- The video concept (or full script if I have one): [WHAT HAPPENS, IN ORDER, FROM SECOND ONE]
- The strongest outcome I am selling: [THE RESULT I LEAD WITH]
- Real, verifiable facts and numbers I can use: [FUNDING, DATES, NAMED INCIDENTS, REAL PRICES, REAL PERCENTAGES. Flag anything that still needs verification.]
- The line I want people to repeat: [THE LAUNCH SUMMARY, IF I HAVE ONE]
</context>

<task>
Evaluate this launch against the principles below, the way the first 300 viewers would, then rewrite the weakest parts and name the single thing most likely to kill it.
</task>

<what_a_winning_launch_does>
Evaluate against these criteria. They make the difference between a launch that blows up and one that dies at 300.

THE CLAIM

- It is bigger than the product. It takes a stance on the category or the world, with the product built inside it, rather than announcing a feature.
- A complete stranger understands the stakes on the first read. If it is only understood by insiders, the ceiling is tiny.
- It is almost unbelievable on purpose, so people stay to see how it could be true.
- It leaves the door open for disagreement. Two different groups can be outraged by it for two different reasons. Agreement is wallpaper. Argument is reach.
- It is anchored in a specific, named, dated, verifiable fact, not a vague grievance.

THE SELL

- It leads with the strongest driver. Money beats time. Status and identity beat both in certain markets.
- It sells the destination the buyer can imagine, not the feature.

THE VIDEO OPENING

- The claim or the craziest moment hits in the first five seconds. No founder story, no corporate montage, nothing about "the journey."
- The most impressive moment is put up front, not saved for second 38.
- It is built to win attention, not for a cinematic edit that assumes a captive audience.

DISTRIBUTION DESIGN

- It aims for the widest possible audience, even for a niche product.
- The product is visible inside the launch itself—something the viewer can actually see working.
- There is at least one clear, memorable line engineered to be quote-tweeted.

THE TEST OF 300

- SCROLL STOP: The first second stops a stranger's thumb.
- COMPREHENSION: Understood in one pass, with no insider knowledge.
- COMMENT TRIGGER: It gives people a reason to comment—an opinion to fight, a specific value to praise, or an open question to answer.
- QUOTABILITY: There is a line worth reposting with an opinion attached.
</what_a_winning_launch_does>

<output_format>

1. THE SCORECARD — score each of the five areas (Claim, Sell, Video Opening, Distribution Design, Test of 300) from 1 to 10, with one honest line of reasoning for each. For the Test of 300, score its four signals separately.
2. CONTEXT LEAPS — list every place where the viewer has to make an inference on their own (knowing the category, the pain, the workflow). Quote the exact phrase.
3. THE WEAK POINTS — the 2–3 lowest scores, with what is wrong specifically.
4. THE REWRITES — the weak parts rewritten with force, shown inline so I can drop them in.
5. THE THING THAT KILLS THIS — the primary reason this launch underperforms, stated plainly.
6. THE VERDICT — one brutal sentence: does this survive the first 300 as planned, or not yet?
</output_format>

<constraints>

- Score honestly. No grade inflation, because politeness here costs me the launch.
- Use only the facts I provided for any rewrite. Never supply a plausible statistic from general knowledge, because an invented number caught in the replies poisons the whole launch.
- Fix the weak points in place rather than just describing the problem.
- For every context leap, name the exact phrase and bridge the gap.
- Write rewrites in plain, human language: vary sentence length, start with concrete nouns and real numbers, end with the strongest sentence. Avoid generic AI fingerprints—words like explore, leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, elevate; the "it's not just X, it's Y" construction; dashes used for drama; and neat summary endings.
- If an input is missing or too thin to judge, write "insufficient signal" for that area rather than guessing, so I know exactly what to define.
</constraints>

## Follow-up when a single line is the weak point

Run at the end of the output:
"Give me 30 variations of this line, ranked, grouped by the emotion each one triggers. Keep the meaning, change the angle."

## Chain
- Input: the founder post draft and the video concept from the brief step.
- Gate: the draft does not freeze at T-24h unless every Test of 300 signal scores 7 or higher, or the owner explicitly accepts the risk.
- Output: the rewrites go back into the founder post draft. The weakest line goes into the 30-variations follow-up, and the owner picks one.
