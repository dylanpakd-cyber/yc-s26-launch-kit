WHEN TO USE: the owner asks "what's our angle/mechanism?", what the market believes, why it works; this skill covers finding the product mechanism and market beliefs that ads build on. WHEN NOT: writing the ad itself (ad-script-and-hooks). One-off asks can load this skill directly; a full run reaches it through yc-launch-ads-workflow.
# SKILL: mechanism-and-market-mining

Purpose: research the market like a strategist and find the mechanism: the plain, true reason the product works, ideally one the market has not heard a hundred times. The output is a ranked concept ladder and a hook bank, every line traced to a source.

## Use when
- The brand pack passed and you need ideas to test.
- The owner says "research the market", "find the mechanism", "what angles are left", "concept ladder", "hook bank", "teardown the competitors".
- Every hook sounds the same as competitors, or a winner is fading and you need new angles.

Not for: reading the owner's own ad results (that is the companion YC Ad Test Desk, which the owner may install).

## Inputs
- brand pack (passed or THIN with the owner's ok).
- Offer type: physical product, software, or information offer. The branch changes the research.
- Market and language.
- What material exists: reviews, calls, ads, a list of competitors.
- Depth: quick (one hour of reading, top three competitors) or full (every real competitor). Default: quick.

## Steps
1. **Kickoff (four questions, one at a time):** offer type; market; what material exists; quick or full.
2. **Open the research block** from yc-launch-ads-references ("Templates") (Research pack section). Nothing lands outside it.
3. **Fifteen-point teardown of the owner's own offer:** offer, price rule, guarantee, policies, trust signals, story, reviews, delivery or access, upsells, social proof, page structure, objections answered, objections missed, claims, tone.
4. **Competitor sweep.** Same fifteen points for each real competitor. Stop when a new competitor adds almost nothing new (roughly when four in five points repeat).
5. **Change over time.** Where public archives allow, note how competitors' prices and messages changed: price rises, new products, new slogans. A message they dropped is a clue.
6. **Review mining.** Collect exact phrases from public reviews and forums, sorted by pain, desire, doubt. Keep misspellings and slang. Each phrase keeps its source link.
7. **Proof ledger.** Every testimonial the owner owns and may use, tagged with the buyer type it convinces. Owned and permitted only.
8. **Ad library heat map.** Competitor ads sorted by how long they have run. Long runners are usually the ones paying for themselves, but this is a signal, not proof. Capture copy, hook, offer and destination.
9. **Keep or flex per long runner:** what must stay for it to work, and what can change.
10. **The mechanism mine.** List the "why it works" claims in the category. Mark each worn out, unowned, or ownable by this product honestly. Pick one primary mechanism. Say it in one plain sentence a stranger would repeat.
11. **Avatar lock.** Three to five buyer types with real quotes. Update the brand pack if new ones appear.
12. **Call mining (optional).** If the owner pastes sales or support call notes, pull objections and phrases. Strip names and contact details first.
13. **Concept ladder.** The five ideas to make first, ranked. Each row: avatar, angle lens, hook type, objection handled, proof available, mechanism, example hook. Rank by proof available first, then by how often the pain appears in customer words.
14. **Hook bank.** As many opening lines as the research supports (aim high, two hundred on a full run), each traced to a phrase or a source. Fewer real hooks beat many invented ones.
15. **Slop sweep and hand-off.** Run the style check and the leak check on the research block. Delete unsourced numbers. Write a short summary: the mechanism sentence, the top five ideas, what to run next.

## Gate
- The mechanism sentence is one sentence, plain words, and true. You can say why it works without a made-up study.
- Every hook and phrase traces to a source. No source, no line.
- The style check and the leak check pass on the research block.
- The ladder has five rows with every column filled, or fewer rows with the reason stated.

## Output
```
MECHANISM: <one plain sentence>
CONCEPT LADDER (ranked)
| # | Avatar | Angle | Hook type | Objection handled | Proof available | Example hook |
HOOK BANK: <count> lines, sources attached
Next: yc-launch-ads-concept-spec on rows 1 to 3
```
Lands in the owner's chat and the research block.

## If things go wrong
- **No public ads for the category.** Use reviews and forums only. Say "no ad library signal" on the ladder.
- **No honest mechanism.** Do not invent one. Use the strongest true angle (proof, comparison, honest admission) and write "mechanism: none owned yet" on the ladder.
- **Competitor claims you cannot verify.** Record them as "their claim", never as fact.
- **Owner wants it fast.** Run quick depth: own teardown, three competitors, twenty phrases, ladder of three.
- **Round cap.** Two passes of research. If the ladder is still thin, ship it with the gaps and the questions that would fill them.

## Never
- Never treat an active or repeated ad as proof it wins. Performance is not public unless the source says so.
- Never scrape private data or log into anyone's account.
- Never put a competitor's brand name in an ad comparison without evidence and the owner's yes.
- Never invent a phrase, a mechanism or a number.

Revision cap: two revision rounds per piece of work. After round 2, stop, show the best version with its open flags, and ask the owner how to go on.
Hand checks: this bot runs no scripts. Every check named in this skill is a written checklist in yc-launch-ads-checks. Apply it by hand, line by line, and write "checked by hand" next to the result.
