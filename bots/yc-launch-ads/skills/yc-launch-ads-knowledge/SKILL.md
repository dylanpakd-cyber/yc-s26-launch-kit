# YC Launch Ads: core knowledge

## Operating law
Drafts only; a typed yes comes before any spend, publish, budget or campaign edit. One banned-words and claims list ({BANNED_WORDS_FILE}); lint every line before showing it. Never invent results, quotes, proof, scarcity or run times. Never search files or other bots for the owner's facts; ask the owner.

## Principles that hold up in practice
1. **Destination beats creative.** Before blaming an ad, open its landing page. If the page no longer keeps the ad's promise (offer, terms, date), results collapse however good the ad is.
2. **A click is not a result.** High clicks with no results is a message-match, offer or traffic-quality problem. Read in order: where the platform spent, then business truth (real sales and costs), then platform diagnostics.
3. **Creative is targeting.** The platform reads the words, voice and picture to decide who sees the ad. An ad for anyone reaches no one in particular; to change who it reaches, change who it speaks to. Still rule out settings, optimization event and eligibility, which cause the same symptoms.
4. **Retargeting is often cheapest; cold demand must be proven by cold traffic.** Copy a retargeting audience's logic, not its headline; retargeting copy often breaks cold-traffic rules.
5. **Keep calls to action off the picture** when one asset serves more than one funnel lane. Use the platform field or a swappable end card.
6. **The hook does most of the work.** Open on a spoken moment or a visible problem and keep the platform's rules; a rule-breaking hook loses before quality matters.
7. **Variation is not iteration.** A new hook, font, first five seconds or rewritten copy is a variation. An iteration starts from a spend-proven winner, keeps most of why it worked, and changes one meaningful thing.
8. **Spend is the platform's vote.** A small ad with a great return is a rumour; a big one is a fact.
9. **Statics prove messages cheaply.** Smoke-test hooks as statics (same image, only the callout changes), then graduate image to motion to video. Reused scenes count as one creative.
10. **Zero spend is not a verdict.** A concept that got no spend next to a dominant winner was never tested. Buy it a clean read if it matters.
11. **Scarcity and proof must be real when the ad runs.** No stale counts, no invented limits.
12. **Gates come before spend.** A test without a written question, variable, success gate and stop gate is spend with a diary.

## Awareness and sophistication
Levels: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most-aware. Cold, low-awareness ads open on a concrete fact, proof or contrast, not the product. Warm and retargeting ads can lead with product, proof, price, offer or action. Sophistication is how often the market has heard the claim; a worn-out claim needs a new mechanism or angle, not louder words.

## Insight-to-truth lock
Six locks (people, position, promise, proof, priority, process), filled from real customer words before any creative: defined in yc-launch-ads-concept-spec step 1.

## Angle lenses (pick one per concept)
Pain moment; failed past solutions; identity; comparison without naming a brand; proof; myth-busting; honest admission; desired outcome day.

## Hook types (write ten, keep three)
Callout; open loop; contrast with the old belief; moment in time; confession; a specific number from real data; the question the viewer already asked; demonstration first; reaction then demo; stakes.

## Body formats (pick one before writing)
Story; analogy; steps; maxim; whiteboard explainer; interview turn; qualifiers (name exactly who it is for). Teaching bodies run what, why, how. Story bodies run "but" and "therefore" between beats. A CTA says who, what, when, how. One CTA, one avatar, one offer.

## Creative formats that repeat across accounts
Reaction then demo; product as a game; a phone notification as the punchline; a recurring mascot; a "what worked for me" note; a spot-the-fake split screen; accidental discovery; one template cloned at volume. A format is proven by repetition across many accounts, not one outlier. Shares and replies say more than likes; saves are a strong intent signal.

## Concept hygiene
- One concept, one reason to buy, one avatar, one test variable.
- Face, age or gender swaps are not new concepts. Start from the problem.
- Remakes copy beat structure one to one; swap only the problem and the product.
- No real living person's likeness without the owner's typed yes.

## Cadence after traction
About 80% of creative effort iterates winning problems and angles; about 20% hunts new angles and avatars. Review top-spend and bottom-spend ads weekly; that weekly read and the test math live in the companion YC Ad Test Desk template, which the owner may install.

Depth: templates, sizing math, claim lanes, the hook library and the worked example are in yc-launch-ads-references; hand checks are in yc-launch-ads-checks.

Revision cap: two revision rounds per piece of work. After round 2, stop, show the best version with its open flags, and ask the owner how to go on.
